
# Self-evaluation — 2025 — Technical Lead

## The WHAT

Over the past year I led work that made our services faster, more reliable, and simpler to operate. I designed and rolled out a new service boundary and moved about 45% of production traffic to it with a zero‑downtime cutover, cutting end-to-end latency by roughly 30%. I also standardized observability across three product teams, reduced noisy alerts by around 60% through better signal and tuning, refactored CI/CD to halve release time and enable canary deployments, and optimized a key batch job to lower memory use by ~25% and shorten median runtime by ~40%.

We faced several cross-team dependencies and legacy-code constraints that slowed early progress. A critical production incident exposed gaps in runbooks and handoffs, so I worked with partner teams to clarify API contracts, add integration contract tests, and update runbooks and on-call rotations to improve incident response and reduce repeated regressions.

To improve delivery and quality I organized architecture reviews, ran focused design workshops, and introduced lightweight decision records for major changes. I tightened code review practices and CI gates, which increased test coverage and reduced rollbacks, and I ran knowledge-sharing and pairing sessions to spread ownership; two engineers took on broader responsibilities and were promoted.

Looking ahead I will focus on reducing mean time to recovery, finishing the remaining service extractions from the monolith, and continuing performance and capacity improvements to lower operational cost. I will also keep mentoring engineers into design ownership so we can scale delivery with confidence.

## The HOW

### Shared Accountability

I organized monthly architecture reviews and ran focused design workshops to distribute ownership and gather diverse perspectives. I established component owners and introduced lightweight architecture decision records (ADRs) for major changes, which improved decision traceability and reduced rework. I partnered with product and QA to align delivery expectations and created clear acceptance criteria for cross-team stories.


### Stronger Together

I coached three engineers through technical design ownership and interview prep; two of them took on broader responsibilities and were promoted. I ran regular knowledge-sharing sessions and pair design days to transfer tribal knowledge and bring junior engineers up the learning curve faster.
