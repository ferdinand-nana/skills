# Skills Definition
Here are the definitions for the skills mentioned above:

### Leading Individuals
1. New Learner. Recognizes individual strengths. Listens actively. Supports growth.
2. Novice. Guides team members with empathy. Provides constructive feedback.
3. Intermediate. Balances autonomy and guidance. Nurtures talent. Facilitates career development.
4. Advanced. Empowers individuals to excel. Coaches, mentors, and champions growth.
5. Expert. Cultivates leadership in others. Orchestrates individual success within the collective vision.

### Communication Skills
1. New Learner. Acknowledges the importance of effective communication in business.
2. Novice. Conveys information clearly and concisely, using appropriate channels.
3. Intermediate. Demonstrates active listening, adapts communication styles, and resolves conflicts constructively.
4. Advanced. Influences stakeholders through persuasive communication, negotiation, and storytelling.
5. Expert. Sets communication standards, shaping organizational culture and driving strategic alignment.

### Innovation
1. New Learner. Aware of the need for creativity and open to new ideas.
2. Novice. Generates novel concepts. Seeks feedback and collaborates on innovative solutions.
3. Intermediate. Proposes and implements creative solutions. Champions experimentation and risk-taking.
4. Advanced. Drives innovation initiatives, fosters a culture of creativity, and disrupts industry norms.
5. Expert. Visionary who revolutionizes markets and shapes the future.

### Coaching & Mentoring
1. New Learner. Understands the role of coaching and mentoring in professional development.
2. Novice. Provides guidance to colleagues or team members on specific tasks.
3. Intermediate. Coaches and mentors individuals, fostering growth, skill enhancement, and career progression.
4. Advanced. Inspires and empowers teams through transformative coaching and mentoring relationships.
5. Expert. Influences organizational culture through impactful coaching and mentoring initiatives.

### Operational Excellence
1. New Learner. Acknowledges the importance of process efficiency and quality.
2. Novice. Participates in process improvement initiatives and adheres to best practices.
3. Intermediate. Leads process optimization efforts, streamlining workflows, and minimizing waste.
4. Advanced. Orchestrates end-to-end operational excellence, fostering continuous improvement culture.
5. Expert. Transforms organizations through lean methodologies, achieving unparalleled efficiency and agility.

### Continuous Improvement
1. New Learner. Recognizes the need for improvement and suggests minor adjustments.
2. Novice. Participates in improvement projects, collects data, and proposes process enhancements.
3. Intermediate. Leads improvement initiatives, applies Lean principles, and fosters a culture of innovation.
4. Advanced. Champions continuous improvement across functions, measures impact, and empowers teams.
5. Expert. Institutionalizes a culture of excellence, integrates improvement into strategy, and achieves breakthrough results.

### Decision Making
1. New Learner. Recognizes the significance of informed decision-making in business.
2. Novice. Makes straightforward decisions based on available information.
3. Intermediate. Evaluates complex scenarios, weighing risks, benefits, and long-term implications.
4. Advanced. Makes strategic decisions, considering organizational impact and future trends.
5. Expert. Drives transformative decisions, influencing industry landscapes and organizational destiny.

### Leading Teams
1. New Learner. Acknowledges team dynamics. Encourages collaboration.
2. Novice. Coordinates team efforts. Sets clear goals. Manages conflicts constructively.
3. Intermediate. Orchestrates team synergy. Aligns diverse talents. Fosters trust and accountability.
4. Advanced. Inspires high-performing teams. Drives innovation. Adapts to challenges.
5. Expert. Architects transformative teams. Shapes culture. Sustains excellence through collective achievement.


### Strategic Thinking
1. New Learner. Understands the concept of organizational vision.
2. Novice. Articulates a basic vision for the team or project.
3. Intermediate. Clearly communicates a compelling vision that inspires others. Aligns team efforts with the vision.
4. Advanced. Effectively communicates and reinforces the vision across the organization. Engages stakeholders and fosters commitment.
5. Expert. Visionary leader. Sets ambitious, future-oriented visions. Mobilizes teams, adapts to change, and drives organizational success.

### Set and Communicate Vision
1. New Learner. Recognizes the importance of strategic thinking. Begins to analyze business challenges and opportunities.
2. Novice. Applies basic strategic thinking. Identifies patterns, considers long-term implications, and aligns actions with goals.
3. Intermediate. Demonstrates strategic acumen. Integrates data, anticipates trends, and develops actionable plans. Balances short-term needs with long-term vision.
4. Advanced. Excels in strategic thinking. Anticipates market shifts, devises innovative strategies, and navigates complexity. Influences organizational direction.
5. Expert. Visionary strategist. Shapes industry landscapes, disrupts norms, and leads transformational change. Inspires others with a compelling vision.
